# Peek Budget

A private, offline-first household income and expense tracker (it started as a subscription tracker). It's a Progressive Web App built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript: no framework, no build step, and no backend apart from an optional sync relay that only stores data it can't read.

- **Private:** your data is stored in your browser's IndexedDB. Optional sync between your devices (**Settings → Sync**, no account, 12 recovery words as the only key) is end-to-end encrypted, so the relay can't read it.
- **Offline:** a service worker caches the app shell, so the app opens and works with no connection.
- **Installable:** add it to the home screen on iOS and Android and it runs like a native app.

## Features

- **Money tab:** at the top, the **Money card**, in the accent colour: this month (or this year in **Annual**), its **Cash due** as the big figure, and **Income** and **Left over** under it, with the **Monthly | Annual** toggle on the card (it drives the Wallet card totals too). Its figures come from the same function as Insights' (`stripSummary()`), so Monthly is Insights' month total and the income its share line is of (the cash due in this month and the income paid in it), and the Calendar's Out and In, and Annual is the Insights yearly card (this year's twelve cash months). It has no "Avg X/mo" line. A Left over below zero has its minus sign and "More going out than coming in". A tap anywhere on the card opens Insights on this month, in the default currency; the toggle, the due-soon chip (see **Due soon**) and the undated line are controls of their own and don't. For screen readers the card is one button, "Cash due this month NT$30,889. Open Insights". Items without a renewal or payment date are left out; one line, "Excludes N undated items", opens the first of them (undated plans first, then income). The card uses the default currency only. Under it, income has its own compact card (see **Income card**), followed by expenses grouped by category. One **View** button in the header (its icon is the view in use) opens a menu of two groups. **View** picks how: **Wallet | Grid | List** (remembered on the device, never synced or exported; Wallet unless another was chosen; a Calendar view saved by 1.19.0 opens as the Wallet, since the calendar is its own tab now). See **Wallet** and **Calendar** below. **Sort groups** orders the groups: **A to Z** (the default, and the order the tab always had, Uncategorised last), **Total** (high to low) or **Items** (most first). Total uses the Money card's cash basis and default currency: this month, or this year in Annual. A group whose items are all in other currencies has no total to compare, so those groups come after the rest, most items first. Ties go A to Z. Income is never one of the groups (it has its own card), so no order moves it. The choice applies to Wallet, Grid and List. It's remembered on the device and never synced or put in a backup. The button says the view and the order in use (its label, and the accent colour when the order isn't A to Z); the menu works with the arrow keys (through both groups), Home, End, Enter and Escape, Android Back closes it, and focus goes back to the button. A pick closes it. Changing the order keeps an open Wallet card open and brings it to the front; with none open the stack goes back to the top. The Money card's three figures and the Wallet card totals **roll** like an odometer, digit by digit: the first time they're shown, when one changes (from the old value), and again when the Money tab comes to the front or the app comes back (from the value shown before if it changed meanwhile, else from 0). A redraw with the same number doesn't roll. Only cards on or near the screen roll, ten figures at most at once across the app (the Money card's three among them). Insights' single figures roll by the same rules (see **Insights tab**). The real text stays in place under the rolling digits, so nothing shifts and screen readers read the final value once; the Wallet card labels are unchanged. With **Reduce motion and effects** the number just changes. Tap any item to edit it, and delete it from the edit sheet after a confirmation. One floating **+** adds items, on Money and Calendar (not on Insights or Settings). It opens the add sheet on an expense, with an **Expense | Income** switch at the top, so it can be switched without losing what you typed (the chosen type is tinted, red for an expense and green for income, and its name says it; so is the "?" tile in the header until there's a name, logo or emoji). The sheet asks for what it needs first: **Name**, **Price** and **Currency**, the cycle, and the date (**Next renewal**, **Next payment** or **Date**, marked "required"; a recurring item can't be saved without one). The rest (website domain, emoji, plan, category, notes) is under **More details**, folded when adding and open when editing an item that has any of them; open or closed isn't saved, and Android Back still closes the sheet in one press. Income shows only **Notes** there: it has no logo, emoji, plan or category in the form, and editing an income item keeps any it already holds. Adding starts with the cursor in **Name** when a hardware keyboard is likely (a key was the last thing pressed, or a mouse); on a touch screen it starts on the sheet itself, so the phone's keyboard opens only when you tap a field; editing starts on the sheet itself; on Calendar it fills in the selected day's date (the date rules are the usual ones). The switch is only there when adding: an item being edited keeps its type.
- **Due soon:** a small chip on the Money card, beside "Cash due", in Wallet, Grid and List (on the Money tab only), counts what comes due in the next 7 days (3 or 14 in Settings): "3 due", with a calendar icon (read out as "3 due in the next 7 days · NT$889 due · +1 in USD"). Tap it for the list, in a sheet (on the overlay stack; Android Back closes it). The sheet starts with what the banner of 1.18.0–1.20.0 said: "3 due in the next 7 days" with the default currency's total due, and a quiet line for "+1 in USD" (other currencies are counted, never added), income coming in when it's included, and "1 item needs a date" (opens the first undated item). Then the rows: today first, then by date and name, each with "Today", "Tomorrow", "In 3 days" or, a week or more ahead, the date, and its amount in its own currency; tap a row to edit the item. It uses the next date as shown everywhere else (a past stored date rolls forward for display; nothing is rewritten or synced), counts a one-off from its own date (today included, never a past one) and lists recurring income ("Next payment") only with **Include upcoming income** on. Days are calendar days on the device's clock, so a DST change can't move "Tomorrow". With nothing due, or with the setting off, the chip isn't there (its line on the card stays, so nothing moves). It has no close button: the banner's hide-for-the-day is gone. **Settings → Due soon**: show the chip (on), the window (7 days) and income (off), per device, never synced or exported. Its figures are plain text (they don't roll), and with **Reduce motion and effects** the sheet opens and closes at once. The calculation is `js/due-soon.js`. Native notifications are not part of it.
- **Calendar:** its own tab, second in **Money | Calendar | Insights | Settings** (a view on the Money tab until 1.20.0): a month grid of due dates. The page is only the calendar, with no Money card, due-soon chip or income card, and Android Back from it goes to Money. It uses exactly the cash basis of Insights and the Money card: a monthly item falls every month, an annual item in the month of its stored date every year, a one-off only in the month of its date, and past and future months are projections from the item dates. Each item falls on its day of the month; in a shorter month a later day is clamped to the month's last day (a monthly item on the 31st falls on the 30th in April and on the 28th or 29th in February, then on the 31st again), the rule `nextRenewal()` uses when it rolls a date forward. So an annual item stored on 29 February falls on 28 February in other years and on the 29th in leap years. From the stored date's month on, the day is `nextRenewal()`'s own; before it, the same clamp. Income (recurring and one-off) is on its day too, marked as income and never added to the expenses. Each day shows up to three dots in the items' group colours (picked or automatic; income in the income green), then "+2"; a dot whose colour is under 3:1 against its cell gets a thin ring rather than a different colour, in light and dark. Days with items have a tinted cell, so the dots are never the only cue; today's number sits in an accent pill and the selected day is outlined. Above the grid: the month with previous and next buttons and **Today**, and the month's cash flow in three columns, a label over each figure: **In** (the income paid in the month, `incomeDue()`), **Out** (its cash due, `cashDue()`) and **Left over** (In minus Out), from the same functions as the Money card and Insights, in the default currency; items in other currencies are counted on one line under them ("+1 in USD"), never added in. Left over shows its size with no sign: an arrow up and to the right when more comes in, down and to the right when more goes out (drawn, not a character), none when they're equal. Only Left over and its arrow are coloured (`--positive-text`, `--negative-text`); In and Out are the page's text colour. It's never called saved or lost: it's the month's cash flow. When the three don't fit side by side (a long currency prefix on a narrow screen) the figures step down a size at a time. Screen readers get one group read as "In NT$40,000. Out NT$80,919. More going out than coming in, NT$40,919" (or "Left over NT$3,200"). Only those three roll (Left over its size; a month step rolls old to new); day cells are plain text. Tap a day for its items under the grid (name, group, amount in its own currency); the day's total is the default currency only, other currencies are counted, never mixed. Tap an item to edit it; tap the selected day again to close the list. Today is selected when the current month is shown. Swipe left or right, or use the buttons, for another month (with **Reduce motion and effects** it changes at once, with no animation). Undated items appear on no day: "1 item needs a date" opens the first. A month with nothing due still shows its grid, with "Nothing due in March 2027". The shown month and day aren't saved. For screen readers it's a grid of rows and gridcells: each day a button such as "5 December 2026, 2 payments, NT$30,390", one tab stop, the arrow keys by a day or a week, Home and End to the week's ends, Page Up and Page Down by a month, Enter to show the day, and the month heading announced once when the month changes. Weekday and month names come from `Intl` in the app's language (`dateWords()` in `js/i18n.js`; zh-TW heads the columns 日 一 二, its single-character names). **Settings → Calendar → Week starts on:** Automatic (the default: the region's first day from `Intl.Locale` week info, else Monday, or Sunday for Taiwan, the US and a few others), Monday or Sunday; per device, never synced or exported. The calculation is `js/calendar-math.js`; its `stepDays()` is the one place that steps through an item's dates. Editing from the calendar (beyond the edit sheet), a year view and custom intervals are not part of it.
- **Swipe between tabs:** a sideways swipe on a page goes to the next tab (swipe left) or the previous one (swipe right), in the tab bar's order, when the finger lifts; there's no wrap-around at Money or Settings, and the tab bar stays as it is (a swipe is only a shortcut, through the same tab change as a tap, so the pill, Back and the rolling totals behave the same). A swipe needs 64px across and at least twice as much across as up or down, so scrolling never switches. It doesn't start while a sheet, menu or open Wallet card is shown, with a second finger, on a form control, within 24px of either side of the screen (the phone's own Back gesture), or on a part with its own sideways drag or scroll: the Ring, the flat donut, the year chart, the currency chips and the Calendar grid (whose swipe changes the month). From the Calendar's In, Out and Left over and its day list, and from a Wallet card (the stack's own drag is up and down only), it does switch. With **Reduce motion and effects** the pill just moves.
- **Wallet:** the Money tab's default view. Each group is a card in a stack, like cards in a wallet: only its top band shows, with the group name, its number of items, its total and up to three logos ("+N" for the rest; a long name beside a wide total, such as "US$26.98" on a narrow phone, takes a second line, broken between words; a card that still has no room for it leaves out its logos, which still show when it's open; the total is always whole); the front card also shows a share bar and its two biggest items. The cards are the expense groups, in the same order as the grid (the order **Sort** picks); income isn't a card (see **Income card**). Card totals are on the same cash basis as the Money card: this month's cash due, or this year's in **Annual**; items in other currencies are left out and counted as "+1 in USD", never converted or added; a group with nothing due says "Nothing due". The cards add up to the Money card's Cash due. Drag or fling to move through the stack (it settles on a card's band; cards that pass the top tilt back into a small pile). Tap a card to open it to all its items while the others slide away; tap an item to edit it; tap the card again, press Escape or Android Back to close it. Each card is a button ("House, 1 item, NT$30,000 this month"); the arrow keys, Home and End move between cards, Enter opens one, and opening and closing are announced. Logos sit on small neutral tiles (white in light, dark grey in dark) so no brand colour meets the card colour; an item without a logo shows its letter on the same tile. Logos load only for cards on or near the screen. Each card's text colour is picked for it (the app's dark ink or white) and its depth (a top-lit gradient, a raised top edge, a short shadow on the card behind, a drop shadow under the front or open card, a faint sheen that shifts as cards move) is fitted so all text keeps at least 4.5:1 everywhere on the card. The stack draws only while it moves. With **Reduce motion and effects** it's a plain list on the page's own scroll, with no tilt or springs, and a card opens and closes at once.
- **Income card:** income has its own compact card under the Money card, in the look of the Wallet's old Income card (white in light, dark grey in dark). It is never a Wallet card or a Sort group: in the Wallet it's pinned above the stack, which scrolls beneath it (the stack measures its room under the header, the Money card and this card, so the last card still clears the +); in Grid and List it's first and scrolls with them. Closed, it shows its title, a gauge and the gauge's words, and no income total (the Money card has it). Tap it for the income items, by name and amount (tap one to edit it; with none yet, **Add income**); open or closed isn't saved. The gauge is a dark neutral track with a segment in the front Wallet card's group colour (the open card while one is open, else the card at the front) for that group's cash due as a share of the income, both in the default currency on the Money card's basis and following **Monthly | Annual**: "House · 75% of income" (under 1% says "<1%"). More than the income fills the bar and says the true percentage ("125%"); with no income this period there's no segment and it says "No income this month"; a group with only other-currency items says so and is never compared; no expense groups, an empty bar. In Grid and List, and in the Wallet with **Reduce motion and effects** while no card is open (a plain list has no front card), the segment is all the outgoings, in the accent colour: "Outgoings · 81% of income". It moves on the same critically damped spring as the stack and jumps with **Reduce motion and effects**. Its words are said once, politely, when the stack stops on a new front card. A segment colour under 3:1 against the track (some of the fifteen automatic and ten picked colours, in each theme) gets a thin light ring, as the calendar's dots do; the colours themselves never change. The maths is `incomeGauge()` in `js/wallet-math.js`.
- **Group colours:** each expense group has a colour from a palette of fifteen (its palette slot), shown as a dot beside its heading in Grid and List, as its card in the Wallet, and in Insights and the Calendar. A default group (Housing, Food, Transport…) takes its own slot; any other group takes the first slot no other group uses, starting where its name points (a hash of the name); past fifteen groups, colours repeat. Slots are shared by the household, so every device draws a group the same; when two devices give two groups the same slot while offline, the later automatic one moves to a free slot after they sync, and a colour someone picked never moves. Tap the dot to pick one of the fifteen (the ones other groups show are marked). Old picks from the earlier ten swatches stay exactly as they were picked. **Sort** never changes a colour; where two neighbouring Wallet cards look close (OKLab ΔE under 26 in normal vision or under colour-blindness simulation), a band of the page colour separates them, in every order. On its first start, 1.23.0 shares the automatic colours each device showed by itself until then (see Storage and backups). Income has no colour.
- **One-off items:** set the cycle to **Once** and pick a date instead of a renewal. They're tagged "One-off" and only count in the month and year of their date.
- **Insights tab:** step through months with the arrows. The month figures are **cash due**: what falls due in that month. A monthly plan counts every month, an annual plan counts its full price in the month of its renewal date (every year), and a one-off counts in the month of its date. The app doesn't record what was paid, so past and future months are both projections from the plan dates. The page leads with the chart: **Month | Year** sits in the header beside the title, the month arrows right under it, then one small line, "N% of income": the month's outgoings as a share of the income paid in it by the same rules (monthly income every month, annual income in the month of its payment date, one-off income in its month). A month with no income shows no percentage and says why ("No TWD income paid in March 2027, so no percentage", or "No TWD income recorded" when there's none in that currency at all). Income and outgoings are compared, never added together. Then the chart (the Ring, the flat donut or the List, whichever was chosen), with the month's total in its centre on one line (in the Ring's hole, measured from the 3D view; at the head of the List). The Ring and the donut sit in a card about 40% of the screen tall at most (never taller than the ring needs), pinned under the header while the legend under it scrolls, so a row's slice turns or lights up right above it; its first rows show on landing, and the page keeps a focused row clear of the card. After the legend, in its own panel, whose last line counts the month's outgoings by type ("4 monthly · 0 annual · 1 one-off"), the notices, then a yearly card for the viewed month's year with **Income**, **Outgoings** and **Left over**, each the sum of that year's twelve cash months (so undated items are left out, as in the month figures; "Excludes N undated items" says so when either notice is showing). A year that spends more than it takes in shows Left over with a minus sign, in the warning colour, with "More going out than coming in"; a year with no dated income says "No income dated in {year}" and shows no left-over figure. Then a ranking, all on the same cash basis. The month's total and the yearly card's Income, Outgoings and Left over roll like the Money tab's figures: when Insights comes to the front, when the app comes back, when a figure changes (stepping to another month rolls old to new), never on a redraw with the same number, and only figures on screen. The share line, per-category amounts, the legend, the Ring's caption and the year chart's readout stay plain text (the readout changes on every frame of a swipe). With **Reduce motion and effects** nothing rolls. Recurring plans without a renewal date can't be placed in a month, so they're left out of the month figures; a notice ("N plans need a renewal date, X not shown", X being their prices added up) opens the first one for editing. Income without a payment date gets its own notice ("N income items need a payment date, X not shown"), which works the same way. The breakdown has a **Ring | Donut | List** toggle (remembered on the device; Ring unless Donut or List was chosen). **Ring** is a tilted 3D ring of the same slices: drag or flick to spin it, tap a slice or legend row, or use the arrow keys, and it settles with a slice at the front. The front slice is raised (its neighbours partly; a very large slice holds its lift across most of its arc), the others are slightly dimmed, and one caption line under the ring names it with its amount and share; the legend marks it, and it is announced to screen readers once the ring stops. Tiny slices are drawn at least 14° wide so they can be tapped; the text always shows the true amounts. It draws only while it moves. When **Reduce motion and effects** is on (Settings; per device, following the system setting until changed), WebGL isn't available, the 3D view loses its graphics context or can't load (offline before its first use), the flat donut stands in with a line saying why, and the arrow keys move its highlight. The donut uses the group colours with the month's total in the centre, and the legend below lists each category's amount and share, largest first. Tap a slice or legend row to highlight it, and tap again to clear. Categories under 3% are pooled into a neutral "Other" when there are two or more of them (a lighter grey in dark mode since 1.22.0, 4.9:1 on the card, so it reads in the Ring). One-offs count in the month of their date, and income is never part of the category views. If you use several currencies, a chip under the share line switches between them. Totals are never converted between currencies.
- **Insights → Year:** a **Month | Year** switch in the header beside the Insights title (remembered on the device). Year shows the year chart, filling the first screen under its controls (as wide as that height allows): every month as a bar of the cash due in it, on an endless loop (December runs into January), with the current month in the middle. The centre month is the widest bar, outlined in the accent colour, with its label in an accent pill; the other months are muted (lower opacity with a veil of the card colour) and fade further towards the edges. No shadows or filters, so it stays cheap on older phones. The y-axis labels sit in a gutter left of the plot, where no bar can cover them. **This year** shows the calendar year; a month drawn past the loop's seam (January to April after December, say) carries its year ("'26"), so the wrap never reads as the next year. **Rolling ±6 months** (the default, remembered) the six months either side of the real current month with the year under each month. Swipe to move (it flings and settles on a month), use the arrow keys, or tap **Jump to now**. The readout over the top of the chart names the centre month (when it needs more lines, on a narrow screen or in a long language, it pushes the chart down rather than run into the scale), its total and how it compares with the average of the months shown (the dashed line, "Average of these months" in the key); a small triangle marks months more than 10% above it. **By type** stacks monthly plans, annual renewals and one-offs; **By category** uses the group colours. The key (what each colour, line and mark means) is behind a small **Show key** toggle under the chart, remembered on the device; an opened month names its parts itself. **Show income** adds a line for the income paid each month and marks months where more goes out than comes in with a small diamond instead of the triangle. Undated items are left out, with one "Excludes N undated items" line, and the yearly card below shows this calendar year. Insights keeps its own room under the tab bar: it has no + button, so the room kept for one doesn't apply. The chart is one tab stop (a list of months with arrow keys); each month has a label such as "March 2027, NT$40,398" and the centre or opened month is announced. With **Reduce motion and effects** on (or, when it has never been set, the system's reduced-motion setting), it moves without inertia and settles almost at once.

Otherwise logos are loaded from `https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain={domain}&sz=128`. - **Category:** a drop-down under the add and edit sheet's **More details** (1.25.0): **Uncategorised** (the default), the eleven default groups in the language in use, the household's other groups A to Z (live expenses only, one per name ignoring case and outer spaces), then **New group…**, which shows a name field under it. Known services: Netflix, Spotify, YouTube Premium, Disney+, iCloud, Google One, ChatGPT, Claude, Adobe, Microsoft 365, Nintendo, PlayStation, Xbox, Notion and Canva. Edit `js/services.js` to add more.
- **Renewal and payment dates:** required for monthly and annual items when you add or edit one: "Next renewal" for an expense ("Pick a renewal date."), "Next payment" for income ("Pick a payment date."). Both are kept in the same `nextRenewal` field. One-offs, expense or income, have a "Date" instead. Dated income on the Money tab says "Payment in 3 days" and so on, never "Renews". Both escape cells that would run as formulas. If the clipboard is blocked, the CSV is shown selected below the row to copy by hand. Each says what it did ("Saved substracker-2026-11-18.csv", "Copied 41 items"), every time it's used. Import checks the whole file first and shows what it will do (for example "12 items, 3 new") before you choose **Merge** (adds new items; for an item already here, whichever copy was edited more recently wins) or **Replace all**. A file with a problem is rejected with a message naming it, and nothing changes.

## Data model

Each item (expense or income) is stored as:

```json
{
  "id": "uuid",
  "type": "expense",
  "name": "Example Streaming",
  "domain": "example.com",
  "emoji": "",
  "plan": "Family",
  "price": 250,
  "currency": "TWD",
  "cycle": "monthly",
  "nextRenewal": "2030-01-01",
  "date": "",
  "category": "Entertainment",
  "notes": "",
  "createdAt": "ISO timestamp",
  "updatedAt": "ISO timestamp"
}
```

- `type` is `"expense"` or `"income"`. Records without it (everything saved before 1.1.0) are expenses.
- `cycle` is `"monthly"`, `"annual"` or `"once"`. One-off items use `date` (YYYY-MM-DD) and leave `nextRenewal` empty. Recurring items leave `date` empty; their `nextRenewal` is the next renewal for an expense and the next payment for income.
- Income uses the same shape. The name says where it comes from (e.g. "My salary"), and `domain`, `plan` and `category` stay empty.
- `emoji` is a single emoji (one grapheme, so flags and skin tones count) or empty. Records without it use the favicon or initial.
- `nextRenewal`, `date`, `category`, `plan`, `domain`, `emoji` and `notes` can be empty strings.

## Storage and backups

All persistence goes through `js/storage.js`; no other file touches IndexedDB or localStorage. The IndexedDB database `substracker` (version 3) has these stores:

- `records`: every expense and income item in a sync-ready form, keyed by `id`: `{ id, fields: { …item fields, del }, ts: { field: stamp } }`. Each field carries the hybrid-logical-clock stamp of its last change, and `del` is the deleted flag. Deleting an item sets `del` to `true` and keeps the record as a tombstone. The app only ever sees live records, in the plain item shape above. One reserved record, `settings`, holds the two shared settings (see below); it never has a `del` field, so it is never live and never appears as an item.
- `outbox`: changes not yet sent to other devices, one op `{ id, rec, f, v, t, dev }` per changed field, keyed by `[rec, f]`, so a newer change to a field replaces its unsent older op. Every save, delete and import writes its ops in the same transaction as the records.
- `meta`: `schemaVersion` (4, and 5 once format 5's first-launch step has been verified; `DB_VERSION` stays 3), `deviceId` (made once per install), `hlc` (the clock), `syncCursor` (the last relay entry applied), `syncConfig` (relay URL, household key, enabled), `lastSyncedAt` and records of the one-time migrations. None of it is exported or synced.
- `settings`: per-device settings, one row per key. `autoCurrency` and `autoGroupColors` are the values the app picked by itself (the locale currency, automatically assigned colours); they only fill gaps and are never synced. Format 5's first-launch step turns this device's `autoGroupColors` into shared palette slots (below), after which new groups get shared slots straight away. The `defaultCurrency` and `groupColors` rows from before shared settings are kept untouched as a fallback.

The default currency and group colours someone actually chooses are shared: they're stored as fields of the `settings` record, with the same ops, stamps and outbox as items. `defaultCurrency` is one field; each group's colour is its own field, `color_` plus a 64-bit hash of the group name (the value carries the name and the swatch), so colour changes to different groups on two devices both survive. For the same field the later change wins. A value the app picked by itself never becomes an op, so a device that never chose a currency can't override the household's, and it adopts the household's as soon as it arrives.
- `items`: the 1.3–1.4 store, kept untouched as a fallback (see below).

The merge rules live in `js/sync-core.js`, a pure module with no DOM, storage or platform code, so web, Android and iOS merge identically. Each field is last-write-wins by stamp, ties go to the higher device id, and applying an op twice or in any order gives the same result. A delete only changes `del`, so an edit made elsewhere doesn't undo it; importing or saving the item again brings it back. A stamp from another device more than `MAX_DRIFT_MS` (24 hours) ahead of this device's clock can't pull the clock further than that, so one phone with the wrong date can't drag every device's clock into the future. The op still sorts by its own stamp, so all devices agree on the result.

Migrations run once and are verified before the app switches over. Versions up to 1.2 kept items in a store called `subscriptions`: on the first load of 1.3.0 they're copied into `items`, and the copy is checked (same count, every id present). The sync-ready step then copies `items` into `records`, stamping each item's fields with its own `updatedAt` and queueing an op per field, and checks the copy (one live record per item that reads back identical, a stamp and a matching op for every field). The shared-settings step then copies the stored `defaultCurrency` and `groupColors` into the `settings` record once, stamped at the time of the upgrade and queued to sync, skipping any field the household already set, and checks that every one arrived; until then settings stay per device as before. Until a check passes, the app keeps using the previous store and says so; an unfinished or unverified attempt is cleared and redone on the next load. Format 5's first-launch step (1.23.0; `migrateFormat` in `js/storage.js`, the pure parts in `js/format-migration.js`) comes last: it compares 1.19.0's date rules (`js/legacy-step.js`) with the shared stepper on every item (next date, calendar days, cash due and income over 25 months, the yearly figure), adopts this device's automatic colours as shared palette slots for groups the household has no colour for (a slot another live group holds probes on; picked colours never move and old `g1`–`g10` picks are never rewritten), and writes this device's version note `app_<hash>` = `{ v, fmt: 5, at }`, in one transaction; then it reads the record back and only then sets `schemaVersion` 5. A failure shows nothing (a count-only console warning), changes nothing before the write and only adds the planned values after it, and the step runs again on the next load; once verified it never runs again. The older stores are left untouched as fallbacks and will be removed in a later release. A few per-device view preferences (theme, reduce motion and effects, tab, wallet/grid/list, period, ring/donut/list, the year chart's key, the due-soon chip's three settings; a day the banner was closed, saved by 1.18.0–1.20.0, is left as it was and no longer read) stay in localStorage; they are never synced or exported.

The app asks the browser for persistent storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`), so the data isn't cleared under storage pressure where the browser supports that.

Export files have this shape:

```json
{
  "app": "substracker",
  "schemaVersion": 5,
  "exportedAt": "ISO timestamp",
  "items": [ … ],
  "settings": { "defaultCurrency": "TWD", "groupColors": { "Entertainment": "w5", "Pets": "g1" }, "groupSlots": { "Entertainment": 4 } }
}
```

Format 5 is format 4 plus an item's `repeat` and the colours' `groupSlots`, each only where present. Every export is checked first (read back by this version and by 1.19.0's rules, and compared with the live items); if the check fails, the file is written as format 4, exactly as 1.19.0 wrote it, and the app says so: "Exported N items in the older backup format. All your items are in it; group colours may come back a little different." (until dismissed).

`items` holds the live (not deleted) items with their ids, in the plain item shape. `settings` holds only `defaultCurrency`, `groupColors` and `groupSlots` (an allowlist, `BACKUP_SETTINGS` in `js/storage.js`, so a setting added later stays out of backups until it's listed). Sync bookkeeping (device id, clock, cursor, outbox, field timestamps), entitlement flags and appearance preferences are never exported, and import ignores any other settings a file contains. `groupColors` maps a category name to a swatch key: a palette slot `w1`–`w15` (the `--wallet-1` … `--wallet-15` tokens in `css/app.css`), with its slot in `groupSlots`, or an old pick `g1`–`g10` (`--group-1` … `--group-10`); files without it import fine and get colours assigned automatically, and a file with no `settings` at all leaves yours as they are. Import restores the two settings as changes like any other, so they sync: **Merge** applies the file's colours for the groups it names, **Replace all** takes the file's colours (clearing others) and its default currency.

Import also reads every older backup: schemaVersion 3 files (1.3–1.4, the same envelope) and 1.0–1.2 files (`{ "format": "substracker", "version": 1 | 2, … }`, with records under `subscriptions` in version 1). It checks each item's required fields and their types: `id` (text), `name` (non-empty text), `price` (a number of 0 or more), `currency` (a 3-letter code), `cycle` (`monthly`, `annual` or `once`), `type` if present (`expense` or `income`), and `date` for one-offs. Unknown fields are ignored, so files from newer versions still import. Merge matches items by `id` and keeps whichever has the newer `updatedAt` (an item deleted here since the backup comes back); Replace deletes (tombstones) every item not in the file and takes the file's default currency and colours (Merge takes its colours only). Both write in one transaction, as ops, like any other change.

Saving the export file goes through `deliverFile()` in `js/files.js`, which is the place to swap the browser download for a native share sheet.

## Sync between devices

Devices in one household can sync through a small relay (`relay/`, a Cloudflare Worker; see `relay/README.md`). It's optional and end-to-end encrypted, and there are no accounts. Your data stays stored on each device.

### Setting it up

**Settings → Sync** opens the Sync sheet.

- **Start sync** creates a household. Before sync turns on, a required step shows the **12 recovery words** (numbered, with **Copy words** and the same words as a QR code). **Start sync** stays disabled until **I've saved these words** is ticked. Then the device claims the household's room on the relay and sends its items. A device that was in a household before gets a warning first: its other devices stay on the old household.
- **Join with 12 words** adds a device. The words can be typed in any case, with any spacing or line breaks, and the old 22-character key still works. Mistakes are named: the wrong number of words, "word N isn't in the list" (with the closest match), or "a word is wrong or out of order" (the checksum). The device then checks the relay. Words with no household get "No household found for these words", and joining never creates a room.
  - An empty device just joins.
  - A device that already has items asks first. **Use the household's data** (the default) replaces this device's items and shared settings with the household's, after one more confirmation. **Export a backup first** is right there. Everything is pulled before anything is written, and the swap of records, outbox, cursor and config is one IndexedDB transaction, so a failed pull or write leaves the device exactly as it was. **Merge this device's data into the household** sends this device's items too. Where an item was edited in both places, the later edit wins.
- Once set up, the sheet shows the status (Up to date, Syncing, Offline or the error), the last sync and the changes waiting to send. **Show recovery words** asks before showing them. It also has **Sync now** and **Turn off sync on this device**, which stops syncing and forgets the words but keeps every item and setting. Closing the sheet or hiding the words removes them from the page.

### Recovery words

The household key is 16 random bytes (`generateKey()` in `js/crypto.js`), stored on the device as 22 characters of base64url. The 12 words (`js/words.js`) are another way of writing the same 16 bytes: BIP-39 English (`js/wordlist.js`, the standard 2048-word list), 128 bits of key plus a 4-bit SHA-256 checksum, 11 bits per word. So a device paired before 1.7 shows its current household's words, and nothing needs pairing again. Anyone with the words can read the household's data. If you lose every device and the words, the synced copy can't be recovered.

### How it works

1. **Keys** (`js/crypto.js`, Web Crypto only). HKDF-SHA256 derives three values from the household key: a room id (128 bits) and a token (256 bits) for the relay, and an AES-GCM-256 key. The relay never sees the household key or the AES key, and can't work back to them.
2. **Envelopes.** Each op (one field change, from the outbox) is sealed as `{ id, wall, c }`. `c` is base64 of a random 12-byte IV plus the ciphertext of the op, padded to 64-byte steps. The op id and the wall-clock part of its stamp are bound as additional authenticated data. A device opening an envelope also checks that the op inside has the same id and the same wall time. Anything that fails is dropped and counted, never applied.
3. **Push.** `js/sync.js` seals the outbox oldest first (at most 200 ops or about 512 KB per request) and POSTs it to the room. The relay stores envelopes in an append-only log with a sequence number. It ignores ids it already has and refuses envelopes stamped more than 24 hours ahead. An op leaves the outbox only if it's still the pending op for its field, because a newer edit made during the push must still go out. Starting a household pushes an empty batch first, which claims the room so other devices can join before anything is added.
4. **Pull.** The device fetches entries after its `syncCursor`, 500 at a time, until it reaches the head. Each page is applied in one transaction: stamps are folded into the clock, ops are merged field by field (last write wins), and the cursor moves. That transaction doesn't include the outbox, so pulled changes are never sent back. Applying is idempotent and order-independent.
5. **When.** Sync runs about 1 s after a local change, on start, when the app becomes visible, when the device comes back online, and every 60 s while visible. While visible, a WebSocket also stays open. The relay only uses it to say "there's something new", and the device then pulls. Only one sync runs at a time, and requests are coalesced. Failures retry with exponential backoff and jitter (2 s up to 5 min). A 401 waits for **Sync now** or a config change. Local saves never wait for sync.
6. **Switching, losing data.** Merging (or setting a different key on the developer screen) resets the cursor and queues every record again, so the household receives this device's items and the settings chosen on it. A relay that has fewer entries than a device has already seen, or has no room at all, gets everything again too.

The relay URL is one constant, `DEFAULT_RELAY_URL` in `js/sync.js`. There's no field for it in the app. The hidden **developer screen** (add `?dev=1` to the URL, or tap the version line at the bottom of Settings 7 times) can override it for testing. It can also set a raw key, enable or disable sync, and disconnect, and it shows where the key is kept. It never fills the key into the page: **Show key** fetches it on demand, and an empty key field on **Save** keeps the stored key. A relay URL must be `https://`, except `http://localhost` / `127.0.0.1` for development.

### Where the household key is kept

Everything that reads or writes the key goes through `js/secure-key.js` (`get`, `set`, `remove`, and `kind`). That covers the sync engine, the Sync sheet, the developer screen, Start, Join and Turn off. The key never appears in exports, logs or the page, except while **Show recovery words** (or the developer screen's **Show key**) is open.

- **Phones (`native-secure`):** the Android Keystore or iOS Keychain, through [`@aparajita/capacitor-secure-storage`](https://github.com/aparajita/capacitor-secure-storage). On Android the plugin encrypts the key with AES-GCM using a key that lives in the Android Keystore. It stores the ciphertext in `shared_prefs/WSSecureStorageSharedPreferences.xml`. On iOS the key goes in the Keychain as `whenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`, never synced to iCloud. The IndexedDB sync config then holds only `keyLocation: 'secure'` and `keyTag`, a random id with no relation to the key that says which household the rest of the config belongs to.
- **Web (`web-indexeddb`):** a browser has no real secure store, so the key stays in the IndexedDB sync config, exactly as before. Anyone who can read this browser profile's site data can read the key.

**Moving the key on phones (once).** On each launch, a key still in IndexedDB is written to secure storage, read back and compared. Only then is it removed from IndexedDB, in the same write that records `keyLocation: 'secure'`. If any step fails, the IndexedDB copy stays and sync works as before, and the next launch tries again. The key is never in neither place, and the household doesn't need pairing again. If writing a new key to secure storage fails (Start or Join), it's kept in IndexedDB and moved later. If the config write after it fails, the secure slot gets its previous value back. If removing the key at **Turn off** fails, the leftover is removed on the next launch.

**A key that can't be read.** If the config says the key is in secure storage but it can't be read, sync shows "Sync needs the 12 words on this device. Join again with your recovery words." This happens when app data is restored from a backup onto a new phone, where the Keystore entry doesn't exist. There are no retries, nothing is deleted, the items stay, and **Join with 12 words** works as usual.

**Android backups.** `allowBackup` is still on, so items and settings are backed up as before. Two rule files leave out the one secure-storage file: `res/xml/backup_rules.xml` (`android:fullBackupContent`, Android 11 and earlier) and `res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml` (`android:dataExtractionRules`, Android 12 and later, cloud backup and device transfer). Its Keystore key can't leave the phone anyway, so a copy would be useless elsewhere. The IndexedDB data (items, and the sync config without the key once it has moved) is still backed up. A key that is still in IndexedDB is backed up with it until it moves on the next launch. IndexedDB's files may also keep a deleted value until the WebView compacts them.

### Scanning the QR code

**Join with 12 words** has a **Scan QR code** button above the words box. It only appears where scanning is possible. The QR code (under **Show recovery words** on another device) holds the 12 words as plain text. A scan fills the box and runs the same checks as typing, and you still tap **Join**. A code that isn't 12 recovery words with a valid checksum shows "That QR code isn't a Peek Budget recovery code." and fills nothing. If the camera is blocked or missing, a message says so, and typing still works.

- **Android:** [`@capacitor-mlkit/barcode-scanning`](https://github.com/capawesome-team/capacitor-mlkit)'s `scan()`, which opens the Google code scanner from Google Play services. The scanner runs outside the app, so the app has no `CAMERA` permission. The first time, the scanner module may need to be downloaded: the app checks `isGoogleBarcodeScannerModuleAvailable`, installs it if needed and says "Getting the scanner ready…". Phones without Google Play services get "Scanning needs Google Play services. Type the words instead."
- **Web and desktop:** `getUserMedia` (rear camera when there is one) and [jsQR](https://github.com/cozmo/jsQR) 1.4.0 (Apache-2.0, vendored as `js/jsqr.js` with its licence in `js/jsqr.LICENSE.txt`). It doesn't depend on `BarcodeDetector`, which Chrome on Windows lacks. Frames are decoded in a canvas that is never added to the page. No frames, images or results leave the device. Every camera track stops as soon as a code is read, **Stop scanning** is tapped, the view changes, the sheet closes or the page is hidden.

To try it locally: run the relay (`cd relay`, `npm install`, `npx wrangler dev`) and open the app on two different localhost ports (each port has its own storage). On each one, open the developer screen and set the relay URL to `http://localhost:8787`, so nothing goes to the real relay. Then use **Settings → Sync**: **Start sync** on one, **Join with 12 words** on the other.

## Languages

Every word the app shows comes from a dictionary: `js/locales/en.js` (English, bundled) through `t(key, params)` in `js/i18n.js`. Keys are namespaced by screen (`wallet.band`, `sync.join.noHousehold`), `{placeholders}` are filled in, and plural forms are `key.one` / `key.other` (and whatever forms a language has; Chinese has only `.other`), chosen with `Intl.PluralRules`. Whole sentences are keys, so a translation can put the words in its own order. A key another locale lacks falls back to English with a console warning; a key missing everywhere shows nothing (and is caught by the tests), never the key itself. `index.html` keeps its English text and marks each piece with `data-i18n`, `data-i18n-attr` (labels, titles, placeholders), `data-i18n-html` (the two install lines) or `data-i18n-native` (the app's wording where the web's mentions the browser).

Two languages exist: **English** and **Traditional Chinese** (`zh-TW`, Taiwan usage, since 1.17.0). English reads exactly as it did in 1.15.0. The language is a per-device choice, like the theme: **Settings → Language** offers Same as device, English and 繁體中文, each language named in its own language. The choice is `substracker.lang` in localStorage (no entry means Same as device). It's never synced or put in a backup, and changing it reloads the app. `document.documentElement.lang` follows the language in use (`zh-TW`, so Traditional glyphs are used).

**Same as device** takes the first of the device's languages that has a locale: exactly, else the same language and script (zh-Hant, zh-HK and zh-MO get zh-TW), else the same language in another script. So for now Simplified Chinese (zh-CN, zh-SG) also gets zh-TW, until a Simplified locale exists. Anything else falls back to English.

Numbers, money and dates keep the device's own conventions (en-GB "18 Nov", en-US "Nov 18") when the app is in the device's language, any variant of it. So a zh-CN or zh-HK phone in zh-TW still formats its own way (Taiwan dollars as NT$30,000). When someone picks a language other than the device's, numbers and dates follow that language: zh-TW on an English phone writes `$30,000` for Taiwan dollars (CLDR's standard in Taiwan, with US$, HK$ and so on for the others) and "2026年11月". The formatters (`money()`, `moneyParts()`, the dates, the year chart's axis, the rolling totals) all take the same formatting locale, so a rolling total always ends on exactly the formatted text. `relativeDays()` in `js/i18n.js` ("Today", "Tomorrow", "In 3 days"; "今天", "明天", "3 天後") words the due-soon sheet. Those are words, so they follow the UI language, not the formatting locale (a zh-CN phone in zh-TW still reads 3 天後).

Sort's A to Z ("依名稱" in Chinese) orders group names the language's way: the device's order in English, as before, and Zhuyin (ㄅㄆㄇㄈ, Taiwan's standard order, `Intl.Collator('zh-TW-u-co-zhuyin')`) in zh-TW, with Latin names after Chinese ones. A locale's collation is the `collation` field of its `LOCALES` entry; its `weekdays` field says how the calendar's column heads name the days (`'short'` unless given; zh-TW `'narrow'`). Item names keep the device's order. Automatic group colours follow the group order, so switching language can re-pick some automatic colours so neighbours stay different. Colours someone chose never change.

Chinese typography lives in the shared styles under `:root:lang(zh-TW)`: the Traditional Chinese UI font after the Latin UI fonts (Noto Sans CJK TC, PingFang TC, Microsoft JhengHei), more line height for running text, and no all-caps tracking. Short lines of facts joined with " · " break at spaces rather than inside a word or a date, and `<em>` stays upright.

### Adding a language

1. Write a glossary first, as `docs/glossary-<tag>.md` (see `docs/glossary-zh-TW.md`): the app's core vocabulary (item, group, category, income, expense, cash due, renewal, Wallet, Insights, Sync, household, recovery words, backup, export, import, replace, merge…) and the conventions (punctuation, spacing, what stays in Latin script). Have a native speaker review it, then use it everywhere.
2. Copy `js/locales/en.js` to `js/locales/<tag>.js` and translate the values. Keep every key and `{placeholder}`, the tags in `…Html` keys, and the JSON names and values in straight double quotes in the import messages: the AI and the app read them. Plural groups get exactly the forms `Intl.PluralRules(<tag>)` has (Chinese: only `.other`; Arabic: six). A separator that is a space in English (`common.sentenceSep`) may be empty. Write invisible characters, such as a no-break space, as ` ` escapes.
3. Keep the `// legal check` marker on the same keys. These keys are legal or privacy wording, and each translation of them needs a professional legal review for that language before it ships. So do the safety-critical confirmations (`form.delete.message`, `import.replace.message`, `sync.turnOff.message`, `sync.replace.message`, the `sync.choice.*.hint` keys).
4. Add one line to `LOCALES` in `js/i18n.js` (its own name, its loader and, if the language sorts differently, a `collation`) and the file to `SHELL` in `sw.js`. Locales other than English load on first use and are precached, so they work offline. If `auto` should map more device languages to it, `resolveLocale()` is the place.
5. If the script needs it, add its typography to the shared styles under `:root:lang(<tag>)`: tokens first (`--font`, `--leading`, `--tracking-caps`), never per screen.
6. `npm test` checks the file has the same keys, the language's own plural forms, the same placeholders and tags, and the legal-check markers. Add a mode for it in `tests/i18n.test.js` (copy `zh`), with the person's data in that language and the Latin words it keeps on purpose. `tests/i18n.html` then walks every key screen at 360, 393 and 320px for raw keys, leftover English and text that runs out of its box.

Never translated: the person's data (item, group and category names, the preset names in `js/services.js`), the 12 recovery words (the BIP-39 English list), the Import with AI prompt and its JSON, CSV column headers and values, backup and sync formats, and currency codes. "Uncategorised" and "Income" stay the stored group keys (group colours are keyed by them); only their labels change. The hidden developer sync screen stays English. Messages written by the relay itself are shown as the relay sends them.

`?lang=xx` in the address shows the pseudo-locale (for development; never saved or offered): every string bracketed, accented and about 30% longer, so text that isn't in the dictionary yet stands out unbracketed and layouts that don't stretch show it. `tests/i18n.html` walks the main screens in it at 320, 360 and 393px.

## Run locally

Service workers need `http://localhost` or HTTPS, so opening `index.html` straight from disk won't work. Serve the folder with the repository's own server (Node built-ins only; it tells the browser never to cache):

```sh
npm run serve
```

Then open <http://localhost:8090>. The port has one setting, `PEEK_PORT` (default 8090), in `scripts/port.mjs`; `scripts/serve.mjs` stops with a message rather than share a port that's taken. Set `PEEK_PORT` to serve on another port, and use that port in the links below. Never 8080 or 8765: other local projects use those.

## Native builds

The same web files also ship as an Android app, **Peek Budget** (`com.tcjabc.peekbudget`), wrapped with [Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com) 8. The web version doesn't need any of this: GitHub Pages still serves the files as they are.

You need Node.js 22 or later, the Android SDK with Platform 36 and Build-Tools 36, and **JDK 21**. The Gradle version Capacitor 8 uses (8.14.3) doesn't run on Java 25, which is what current Android Studio bundles, so point `JAVA_HOME` at a JDK 21 for the build. One way: in Android Studio, **Settings → Build, Execution, Deployment → Build Tools → Gradle → Gradle JDK → Download JDK…**, version 21.

```sh
npm install
npm run sync:web      # copy index.html, manifest.json, sw.js, js/, css/, icons/ into www/, then cap sync
cd android
./gradlew assembleDebug   # on Windows: .\gradlew.bat assembleDebug
```

The debug APK is written to `android/app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`. Run `npm run sync:web` again after any change to the web files. `npm run assets` regenerates the icon and splash from `assets/`.

How the native app differs from the PWA:

- **Backups:** Export opens the share sheet (save to Files or Drive, or send it) instead of downloading.
- **No service worker:** the files are inside the app, so it works offline anyway, and updates come with app updates.
- **Separate data:** the app keeps its own storage, separate from the PWA. To move your items, export a backup in one and import it in the other.
- **Status bar:** the app draws behind the status bar, and its icons follow the app's light or dark appearance.
- **Sync key:** kept in the Android Keystore (see "Where the household key is kept").
- **QR scanning:** through Google Play services' code scanner; no camera permission.
- **Back button (Android):** Back closes only the topmost open overlay, the same way its own Cancel, Back or X does. That covers a sheet, a step inside the Sync sheet, a confirmation, the colour picker and the year chart's focus mode. A confirmation's Cancel is its first, least destructive choice, so Back on "Replace all" cancels. While the Sync sheet is busy, Back does nothing. With nothing open, Back goes to the Money tab, and from Money it sends the app to the background (`minimizeApp`, not `exitApp`, so anything still running carries on and the app reopens as it was). Every overlay registers in `js/overlays.js` when it opens. The web and the PWA don't listen for Back, so the browser's own Back is unchanged.

Native plugins: `@capacitor/app` 8.1.2 (the Back button), `@capacitor/filesystem`, `@capacitor/share`, `@capacitor/status-bar`, `@aparajita/capacitor-secure-storage` 8.0.1, `@capacitor-mlkit/barcode-scanning` 8.2.2 (all on Capacitor 8). The scanning plugin brings ML Kit, CameraX and Google Play services libraries into the APK. The debug APK grew from about 5 MB to about 28 MB, mostly ML Kit's bundled barcode model for four CPU types, which `scan()` doesn't use. Those libraries also add `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` and Google's `datatransport`, which ML Kit uses for usage reports.

**iOS notes** (there's no iOS project yet). When one is added with `npx cap add ios`:

- `NSCameraUsageDescription` must be in `ios/App/App/Info.plist`, because ML Kit scanning on iOS uses the camera inside the app. For example: "Scan the recovery-words QR code from another device."
- `@capacitor-mlkit/barcode-scanning` only supports CocoaPods on iOS, not Swift Package Manager (Capacitor 8's default for new iOS projects). Create the iOS project with CocoaPods, or pick a different iOS scanner.
- The secure-storage plugin needs no Info.plist entry and no Keychain Sharing entitlement. The key is stored per device (`whenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`), so it isn't in iCloud Keychain or in backups restored to another iPhone.

Release signing isn't set up. Keystores, `keystore.properties` and `local.properties` are ignored by git and must never be committed.

## Deploy to GitHub Pages

All paths are relative, so the app works from a project subpath such as `https://<user>.github.io/substracker/`.

1. Create an empty repository named `substracker` on GitHub.
2. Push this folder to it:
   ```sh
   git add .
   git commit -m "Initial commit"
   git branch -M main
   git push -u origin main
   ```
3. On GitHub, open **Settings → Pages**.
4. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**, then select the `main` branch and the `/ (root)` folder. Click **Save**.
5. Wait a minute or two, then open `https://<user>.github.io/substracker/`.

The empty `.nojekyll` file tells GitHub Pages to serve the files as they are, without running Jekyll.

### Publishing updates

The service worker serves the cached app shell first. When you change any file:

1. Bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` (for example `v1.0.0` → `v1.0.1`). Also bump `APP_VERSION` in `js/app.js` so the Settings footer shows the new version.
2. Commit and push.
3. The next time the app is opened online, it downloads the new version in the background and shows **"A new version is ready. Reload"**.

If you add a new file to the app shell, also add it to the `SHELL` list in `sw.js`.

## Install on a phone

- **iPhone / iPad (Safari):** open the site, tap **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the site, tap the **⋮** menu, then **Install app** (or use the **Install app** button in Settings).

## Backups

Your data is stored in the browser on that device (and, if you set up sync, on your other devices too). Clearing site data, or uninstalling the home-screen app on iOS, deletes it. Use **Settings → Export** regularly and keep the JSON file somewhere safe. To move data to another device, export on one and import on the other.

## Privacy note

Unless you turn on sync, the only network requests the app makes are for its own files and for favicon images from Google. In the Android app, Google Play services' code scanner and the ML Kit libraries may also send Google usage reports when you scan (no images are sent). On the web, scanning runs entirely in the page. With sync on, it also talks to the relay you set, which only receives encrypted changes (see `relay/README.md` for what it can and can't see). A favicon request includes the domain you entered (for example `example.com`), but never your prices, plans or notes. Leave the domain blank if you'd rather not send it.
